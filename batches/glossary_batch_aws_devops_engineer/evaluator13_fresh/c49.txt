
### 49 aws.sts_session
Inline: session lifetime: The bounded validity interval of temporary role credentials.
tb1: An operator assumes an approved role for a bounded session. The returned credentials have three fields and an expiry, and the permissions explanation must keep role-policy grants distinct from resource-based grants.
tb2: Temporary AWS credentials consist of an access key ID, secret access key and session token and expire after their issued lifetime.
tb3: Call aws sts assume-role with --role-arn, --role-session-name and the approved --duration-seconds, then use all three returned credential fields and inspect Expiration.

Permissions granted through a role’s identity-based policies are intersected with any session policies; resource-based grants are evaluated separately, and grants directly to the session ARN are not limited by session policies; an explicit deny in any applicable policy overrides an allow.
tb4: Substitute the approved role ARN, session name and duration in the assume-role command. Use the returned access key ID, secret access key and session token together, and inspect Expiration. If a session policy is supplied, role identity-policy permissions are intersected with it. Do not extend that sentence to every resource grant: resource-based grants are evaluated separately, and grants directly to the session ARN are not limited by session policies. An applicable explicit deny still overrides an allow. Record the issued lifetime and the supplied permission evidence without displaying credential values. This example teaches session composition and the stated grant distinctions, not a new policy intended to exercise every possible permission case.
tb5: Temporary credentials are not just an access-key pair: the session token is also required, and the issued lifetime ends. Likewise, session-policy intersection describes role identity-based grants; it is not an unqualified limit on all resource-based grants.
