tb1: One requirement asks for more throughput during busy periods. Another asks for service to continue after a component fails. Both involve resources, but they ask different questions. Separate the amount of capacity from the independence of the capacity that remains after a failure.
tb2: Redundant components and multiple locations can keep a workload available when a component fails.
tb3: Capacity must accommodate workload demand; adding or removing resources helps match capacity to changing demand.

For a component-failure constraint, evaluate independent redundant capacity rather than merely increasing the size of the same component.
tb4: Suppose a lab's demand requirement says it must handle 200 requests per second, while its failure requirement says it must keep serving after one component is lost. Increasing the size of a single component may address demand, but evaluating the failure requirement calls for independent redundant capacity. Write two conclusions: whether total capacity accommodates the supplied demand, and whether remaining independent components can meet the supplied service requirement after the loss. Adding or removing resources helps match changing demand; redundant components and multiple locations can support availability during a component failure. Keep the two requirements visible when comparing proposals, rather than accepting a larger component as the answer to both.

Now compare two illustrative proposals with equal total capacity: one larger component and two independent components. Under ordinary load they may both satisfy the supplied demand requirement. Under loss of the single larger component, its capacity is unavailable; with independent components, remaining capacity can be evaluated against the service requirement. Equal total capacity therefore does not settle the failure requirement. The record needs the surviving capacity question as well.
tb5: More capacity in the same component is not the same as independent redundant capacity. For a component-failure constraint, evaluate the latter; for changing demand, evaluate whether the workload has enough capacity. One measure does not replace the other.
