tb1: Audit logs need tamper evidence as well as storage controls. CloudTrail digest validation checks the delivered log interval; it does not itself prevent someone from accessing or deleting the files.
tb2: CloudTrail log-file integrity validation uses signed digest files and hashes to detect modification or deletion of delivered log files.
tb3: Run aws cloudtrail validate-logs --trail-arn ARN --start-time TIME --end-time TIME --region REGION against the supplied log/digest interval and inspect validation failures; for an organization trail also provide the required --account-id.

Digest validation supplies tamper evidence but does not itself prevent unauthorized access or deletion.
tb4: Substitute the supplied trail ARN, start/end times and Region into aws cloudtrail validate-logs. For an organization trail, include the required --account-id for the supplied account. Inspect validation failures for that log/digest interval. The validation uses signed digest files and hashes to detect modification or deletion of delivered logs. Keep the interval and validation result in the evidence record rather than declaring all logs intact from an unrelated successful interval. A passing validation result is tamper-evidence verification for the checked material, not an authorization control or prevention of future deletion.

Keep validation scope explicit in the result: trail identity, account when required, selected interval and Region. A failure is evidence to preserve and investigate under the supplied procedure, not a reason to omit the affected files from the record. Authorization and retention still need their own controls because knowing that material was changed or deleted is not the same as preventing that action.
tb5: Digest validation detects tampering; it does not prevent unauthorized access or deletion. Run it on the supplied log/digest interval and preserve failure evidence, keeping that role distinct from access and retention controls.
