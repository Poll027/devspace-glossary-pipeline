tb1: A delivery report shows deployments are more frequent, but recovery evidence is also changing. Read throughput and instability together before proposing a conclusion about the delivery workflow.
tb2: Deployment frequency measures how often changes are deployed; change lead time measures the elapsed time for a committed change to reach production.
tb3: Change fail rate measures the proportion of deployments requiring intervention; failed deployment recovery time measures how long recovery from a failed deployment takes.

Compare throughput and instability measures together over time and within application context rather than using one measure to rank individuals.
tb4: Suppose one period has more deployments and shorter commit-to-production time, but a larger proportion of deployments need intervention. The first observations concern deployment frequency and change lead time; the latter concerns change fail rate. Add the failed-deployment recovery-time evidence to understand how long those failures take to recover from. Compare these measures over time for the same application context. Do not turn one improving measure into a ranking of individual operators.
tb5: Frequent delivery alone does not describe stability. Keep failure proportion and failed-deployment recovery time beside throughput measures, and interpret the combined evidence within the application's context.
