
### 24 aws_devops.choose_rolling_blue_green_or_canary_delivery_for_stated_availability_and_rollback_requirements
INLINE supplied service constraints=supplied; rolling versus blue/green versus canary=Rolling replaces incrementally; blue/green uses parallel environments and a traffic switch; canary exposes a bounded portion of traffic first.; supplied strategy scenario=supplied
f1 Rolling delivery replaces portions of the workload progressively rather than replacing all instances simultaneously.
f2 Blue/green delivery maintains separate old and new environments and switches traffic to the new environment.
f3 Canary delivery exposes a new version to a limited share of traffic before broader rollout.
f4 Choose a strategy by comparing traffic exposure, available parallel capacity and the ability to return traffic to a known-good version.
tb1 A release requirement allows only limited exposure to a new version and needs a way to return traffic to a known-good version. Compare delivery strategies by their replacement and traffic behavior, not by assuming one is always safest.
tb4 Consider three proposals for the same approved release. Rolling replaces portions progressively. Blue/green maintains separate old and new environments and switches traffic. Canary exposes the new version to a limited traffic share before a broader rollout. Compare the supplied exposure constraint, available parallel capacity and rollback requirement with each proposal. If the requirement calls for separate old and new environments, the blue/green description directly addresses that arrangement. If it calls for limited initial traffic exposure, examine the canary proposal against that limit. Record how traffic can return to the known-good version under the supplied strategy instead of declaring that the strategy name guarantees recovery. The comparison is about stated requirements, not a universal ranking.
tb5 Rolling and canary are not simply two names for the same exposure pattern. Rolling describes progressive workload replacement; canary describes limited traffic exposure before broader rollout. Blue/green additionally uses separate old and new environments and a traffic switch.

### 26 aws_devops.configure_cloudwatch_alarm_evaluation_and_missing_data_behavior_from_supplied_signals_and_thresholds

f1 A metric alarm evaluates a statistic over periods against a threshold and has OK, ALARM and INSUFFICIENT_DATA states.
f2 EvaluationPeriods is the number of evaluated periods, and DatapointsToAlarm is the number of breaching datapoints required within that window.
f3 Set the supplied metric, statistic, period, comparison operator and threshold along with the evaluation counts; compare the resulting state with the supplied samples.
f4 Missing-data settings are breaching, notBreaching, ignore (retain current state), and missing (insufficient data when all datapoints are missing); missing is the default.
tb1 An alarm should reflect a supplied sequence of metric samples, including gaps. To explain its state, separate the statistic and threshold from the size of the evaluation window and the number of breaches required within it.
tb4 Suppose the approved samples for three evaluated periods are above, below and above the supplied threshold. Set EvaluationPeriods to 3 and DatapointsToAlarm to 2 for this example, alongside the supplied metric, statistic, period and comparison operator. There are two breaching datapoints in that window, matching the stated count requirement. Now examine the supplied missing-sample case with its selected missing-data policy. Do not silently treat a gap as a real zero or a real breach: use breaching, notBreaching, ignore or missing as configured. For the all-missing case, missing yields insufficient data; ignore retains the current state. Compare the resulting state with the supplied cases and retain the settings with the sample evidence.
tb5 EvaluationPeriods is not the number of breaches required. It is the number of evaluated periods; DatapointsToAlarm supplies the required breach count. Also, missing is the default missing-data setting, not a promise that every isolated gap immediately produces INSUFFICIENT_DATA.
