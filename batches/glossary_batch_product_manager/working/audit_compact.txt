### 11 customer_research.market_sizing PREREQS []
f1 literal_taught=True: Total Addressable Market estimates the total potential market opportunity for a product or service.
f2 literal_taught=True: A top-down estimate narrows a broad market using external industry data and relevant constraints.
f3 literal_taught=True: A bottom-up estimate uses the number of potential customers and annual revenue per customer to estimate annual market revenue.
f4 literal_taught=True: Document the customer-count and revenue assumptions and, for the obtainable market, the time horizon and assumptions about willingness to buy, marketing and distribution reach, and competition.
f5 literal_taught=True: The Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM) is the portion of TAM within geographical reach; estimate its annual revenue by multiplying the reachable potential-customer count by annual revenue per customer.
f6 literal_taught=True: The Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM) is the portion of SAM realistically achievable; calculate it as SAM multiplied by an assumed achievable share for the chosen period, treating that share as an estimate rather than guaranteed demand.
tb1: Someone says the market is worth millions, but the team does not know which customers the figure includes or what revenue it assumes. Market sizing turns that broad claim into estimates with visible boundaries. Keep the total opportunity, the geographically reachable portion, and the realistically obtainable portion distinct.
tb4: Use invented annual figures. Suppose 10,000 potential shops could each generate $120 per year. A bottom-up TAM estimate is 10,000 × $120 = $1,200,000 in annual market revenue. If 4,000 of those shops are within geographical reach, SAM is 4,000 × $120 = $480,000 annually. If you assume the business could achieve 5% of that SAM within a chosen one-year horizon, SOM is $480,000 × 0.05 = $24,000 annually. This corresponds to 200 shops under the same revenue assumption. State why you assumed that share, including willingness to buy, reach through marketing and distribution, and competition. None of the multiplication establishes that those shops will actually purchase.
tb5: A top-down estimate would instead begin with external industry data and narrow it using relevant constraints. In either approach, make the customer boundary and annual revenue assumptions visible so the reader knows what is being estimated. Do not mix monthly revenue per customer with an annual market total. If the assumed obtainable share changed to 2%, the example SOM would be $9,600, showing how strongly the result depends on the assumption. The time horizon belongs beside the obtainable estimate, not hidden behind a seemingly precise currency number.
