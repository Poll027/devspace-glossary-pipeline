FACT f1 The range of a data set is the largest value minus the smallest value.
FACT f2 The variance is computed by taking each observation's deviation from the mean, squaring it, summing the squared deviations, and dividing by n - 1 (one less than the number of observations); it is roughly the average squared distance of the observations from the mean.
FACT f3 The standard deviation is the square root of the variance, and it describes the typical distance of an observation from the mean.
FACT f4 The standard deviation is expressed in the same units as the original data, while the variance is expressed in squared units; the range is based only on the two most extreme values and ignores how the rest of the data are spread.
tb1 Suppose dispatch-preparation times are 2, 4, and 6 minutes. Their mean is 4. The range is 6 − 2 = 4 minutes. For the sample variance, subtract the mean from each observation to get -2, 0, and 2; square these deviations to get 4, 0, and 4; then sum to 8.
tb2 The range of a data set is the largest value minus the smallest value.
tb3 The variance is computed by taking each observation's deviation from the mean, squaring it, summing the squared deviations, and dividing by n - 1 (one less than the number of observations); it is roughly the average squared distance of the observations from the mean. The standard deviation is the square root of the variance, and it describes the typical distance of an observation from the mean. The standard deviation is expressed in the same units as the original data, while the variance is expressed in squared units; the range is based only on the two most extreme values and ignores how the rest of the data are spread.
tb4 Divide by n − 1, which is 2 for three observations: variance is 8 / 2 = 4 square minutes. Standard deviation is the square root of that variance, or 2 minutes. The different units matter: variance is squared, while standard deviation uses the original minutes and describes typical distance from the mean.

Range only uses the smallest and largest values. A second sample, 2, 4, 4, 4, and 6, has the same range, but its variance is 8 / 4 = 2 square minutes and standard deviation is about 1.41 minutes. The added central observations change the spread calculation without changing the extremes.

The practical reporting point is that range alone does not describe how the rest of the data are spread. State which measure you use and its units. The variance formula here is the sample form with n − 1; keep that denominator instead of silently switching to another convention.
tb5 Do not divide this sample-variance calculation by n: the stated denominator is n − 1. Standard deviation is not the variance itself, and it is not in squared units. Equal ranges also do not prove equal spread among all observations.
