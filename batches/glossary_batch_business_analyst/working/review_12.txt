tb1 Imagine the business outcome is fewer dispatch delays. A functional requirement supporting that outcome might be “Display the current delivery instructions when a dispatcher selects an order.” This describes what the deliverable must do, rather than restating the outcome or saying how quickly it should operate.
tb2 Functional requirements state what the deliverable must do.
tb3 Document the expected functions separately from the broader business outcome and from how the solution should perform. Write a detailed functional specification entry with the initiating event and input values, the expected system response, resulting outputs, and applicable preconditions, postconditions, or exception behavior.
tb4 Expand it into a hypothetical specification entry. The initiating event is selecting an order; the input is the selected order identifier. The expected response is to retrieve and display that order’s current instructions, and the output is the displayed instruction text. A precondition could be that the order exists; a postcondition could be that its current instructions are visible. For an unknown identifier, the example exception behavior is to display a clear not-found result instead of instruction text. These are example requirements, not claims about an existing product.

The detailed entry now gives a reader the behavior for both the ordinary situation and the relevant exception. Keep the broader business outcome in its own place and document performance expectations separately. “Reduce delays” explains why the work matters, but it does not tell the reader what happens when an identifier is selected.

The practical skill is moving from a vague function label such as “instruction access” to an explicit event, inputs, response, outputs, and applicable conditions. Do not invent preconditions or exceptions silently; in real elicitation those details need to be established for the requirement.
tb5 A functional specification is not a list of business aspirations. Nor does “fast lookup” fully describe the function. Specify the expected behavior and applicable conditions separately from the broader outcome and the way the solution should perform.
