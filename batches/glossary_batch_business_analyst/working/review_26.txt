tb1 Imagine the current dispatch map includes a separate instruction-sheet lookup and a clarification request. The team proposes a future process in which current instructions are available at the order step. A to-be map shows that intended future behavior, not a claim that the change has already happened.
tb2 A future-state process map shows how the process is intended to work after improvement.
tb3 Use the current-state map to identify improvements and draw a revised map reflecting the proposed changes. Use BPMN tasks, events, gateways, and sequence flows to represent the revised activities and routing.
tb4 Use the current-state map to identify the proposed improvements. In this hypothetical redesign, replace the separate lookup with “Read current instructions on order,” and revise the routing for clarification according to the proposed behavior. Draw the changed activities using BPMN tasks, appropriate events and gateways, and sequence flows. Keep the revised map understandable as a complete proposed process rather than a few isolated change labels.

Read the old and revised sequences separately. The as-is sequence records the current lookup; the to-be sequence reflects the proposed instruction-access change. Their different meanings matter even when both use the same BPMN shapes.

The useful output is a future-state map that explicitly represents intended activities and routing after improvement. The example is a proposal, not evidence of achieved efficiency. A revised diagram can communicate how the process is meant to work while leaving implementation and actual results to their own work.
tb5 To-be does not mean already operating. Do not describe a proposed route as observed behavior or assume that redrawing a process proves an improvement has been achieved. The model represents the intended future state.
