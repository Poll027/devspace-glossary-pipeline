Product Owner accountability includes maximizing product value and effective backlog management. Some backlog-management work can be delegated, but that does not transfer the accountability.
tb2 The Product Owner is accountable for maximizing product value and effective backlog management.
tb3 The Product Owner may delegate backlog-management work but remains accountable for it. Developers are responsible for sizing the work and deciding how selected work is performed. Clarify backlog items collaboratively while respecting the Product Owner’s ordering accountability and the Developers’ delivery decisions.
tb4 In this hypothetical collaboration, the BA drafts clearer item wording and brings stakeholder questions to the group. The Product Owner remains accountable for ordering. Developers size the work and decide how selected work will be performed. The BA’s useful contribution is clarification that helps these decisions, not taking them over.

If a stakeholder asks the BA to promise a size or dictate the implementation approach, distinguish the BA’s requirements contribution from Developers' responsibility. If the BA updates backlog detail under delegated work, distinguish doing that task from becoming accountable for product value and backlog management.

The practical mental model is collaboration with explicit boundaries. People can contribute to the same item while retaining different accountabilities and delivery decisions. Clear descriptions support collaboration, but they should not be presented as authority to impose order, size, or implementation on the people accountable for those decisions.
tb5 Delegating backlog-management work does not transfer the Product Owner’s accountability. Nor does preparing requirements make the BA responsible for sizing or deciding how selected work is performed. Collaborate on clarity while respecting ordering and delivery boundaries.
