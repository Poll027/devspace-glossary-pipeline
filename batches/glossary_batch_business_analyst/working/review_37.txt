FACT f2 A one-to-many relationship has a column with unique values on one side and potentially repeated matching values on the other.
FACT f3 A many-to-many relationship permits repeated matching values on both sides.
FACT f4 Determine the relationship by checking whether each side permits one or multiple matching instances.
FACT f8 In crow’s-foot notation, a circle (ring) represents zero, a bar (dash) represents one, and a crow’s-foot represents many.
FACT f9 Pair a circle and bar for optional zero-or-one, two bars for mandatory exactly-one, a circle and crow’s-foot for optional zero-or-many, or a bar and crow’s-foot for mandatory one-or-many.
FACT f10 At each relationship end, place the minimum symbol inward and the maximum symbol closest to the entity box.

tb1 Suppose business rules say a customer may have zero or many orders, while each order must relate to exactly one customer. Cardinality expresses permitted counts, not merely the number of rows currently in a sample.
tb2 Relationship cardinality expresses how many instances on either side can be related.
tb3 A one-to-many relationship has a column with unique values on one side and potentially repeated matching values on the other. A many-to-many relationship permits repeated matching values on both sides. Determine the relationship by checking whether each side permits one or multiple matching instances. In crow’s-foot notation, a circle (ring) represents zero, a bar (dash) represents one, and a crow’s-foot represents many. Pair a circle and bar for optional zero-or-one, two bars for mandatory exactly-one, a circle and crow’s-foot for optional zero-or-many, or a bar and crow’s-foot for mandatory one-or-many. At each relationship end, place the minimum symbol inward and the maximum symbol closest to the entity box. Draw a relationship line between the entities and annotate both ends with the symbol pair for the number of instances of that end’s entity that can relate to an instance of the other entity.
tb4 Check each direction: one customer can match multiple orders, and one order matches one customer. In crow’s-foot notation, place circle plus crow’s-foot at the Order end for zero-or-many orders per customer. Place two bars at the Customer end for mandatory exactly-one customer per order. At each end, put the minimum symbol inward and the maximum symbol closest to the entity box.

The symbols carry separate meanings: circle means zero, bar means one, and crow’s-foot means many. Circle plus bar would mean optional zero-or-one; bar plus crow’s-foot would mean mandatory one-or-many. Use the pair appropriate to the actual business rule instead of choosing whichever shape fits the available space.

The data perspective also helps: one-to-many has unique values on one side and potentially repeated matching values on the other; many-to-many permits repeated matching values on both sides. Determine the relationship by checking whether each side permits one or multiple matching instances. The example’s mandatory order-to-customer rule is a hypothetical business rule for this diagram, not inferred solely from observing one current order.
tb5 A relationship end describes how many instances of that end’s entity can relate to an instance of the opposite entity. Reading the crow’s-foot as the count of customers instead of orders reverses the example. A current sample also does not by itself establish all permitted counts.
