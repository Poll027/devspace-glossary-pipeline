FACT f1 RIGHT OUTER JOIN returns matching combinations and preserves each unmatched right-table row.
FACT f2 For an unmatched right row, columns from the left table are filled with NULL.
FACT f3 Write SELECT a.column_name, b.column_name FROM table_a AS a RIGHT OUTER JOIN table_b AS b ON a.key_column = b.key_column; with the table to preserve on the right.
tb1 Use a hypothetical report where Orders contains (10, 101) and (11, 101), and Customers contains (101, North Stores) and (102, South Stores). You want all customers represented even if a customer has no orders. With Customers placed on the right, a right outer join preserves that side.
tb2 RIGHT OUTER JOIN returns matching combinations and preserves each unmatched right-table row.
tb3 For an unmatched right row, columns from the left table are filled with NULL. Write SELECT a.column_name, b.column_name FROM table_a AS a RIGHT OUTER JOIN table_b AS b ON a.key_column = b.key_column; with the table to preserve on the right.
tb4 Write:
SELECT a.OrderID, b.CustomerID FROM Orders AS a RIGHT OUTER JOIN Customers AS b ON a.CustomerID = b.CustomerID;

The matching results include (10, 101) and (11, 101). Customer 102 has no matching order, so the result also includes (NULL, 102). The missing values are from the left table, Orders, because this is an unmatched right-table row.

Compare table position with result interpretation. It is Customers that must remain represented for this business question, and Customers is on the right of RIGHT OUTER JOIN. The join returns matching combinations as well as preserved unmatched right rows. It does not imply that every right row has a real order.

The practical skill is choosing and reading preservation correctly. A report reader seeing NULL for OrderID should understand that the preserved customer lacked a matching left-table row under this condition. Do not turn that missing match into a fabricated order number.
tb5 Right outer join preservation follows the right-hand table in the query. For an unmatched right row, left-table fields are NULL. That is the opposite missing-side placement from an unmatched left row in a left outer join.
