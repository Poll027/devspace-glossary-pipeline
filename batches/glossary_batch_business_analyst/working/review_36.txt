FACT f1 A many-to-many (M:N) relationship cannot be implemented directly in the relational model; it is resolved into two one-to-many relationships using a composite entity, also called a junction or bridge table.
FACT f2 The junction table must contain at least the primary keys of the two original tables, used as foreign keys, and may also contain additional attributes of its own.
FACT f3 The combination of the two original tables' primary keys, brought in as foreign keys, will make the primary key of the junction table (or, together with another attribute, can form it).
tb1 Imagine orders can include several products, and a product can appear in several orders. That is a many-to-many relationship. In the relational model described here, resolve it using a junction table rather than attempting to implement the many-to-many relationship directly.
tb2 A many-to-many (M:N) relationship cannot be implemented directly in the relational model; it is resolved into two one-to-many relationships using a composite entity, also called a junction or bridge table.
tb3 The junction table must contain at least the primary keys of the two original tables, used as foreign keys, and may also contain additional attributes of its own. The combination of the two original tables' primary keys, brought in as foreign keys, will make the primary key of the junction table (or, together with another attribute, can form it).
tb4 For this hypothetical model, create OrderProduct between Order and Product. It contains OrderID and ProductID, brought in from the original primary keys and used as foreign keys. It may also contain its own attribute, such as quantity. The two original tables now each have a one-to-many relationship with OrderProduct.

Example junction rows are (Order 10, Product A, quantity 2), (Order 10, Product B, quantity 1), and (Order 11, Product A, quantity 3). Order 10 links to two products, and Product A links to two orders. The pair of original identifiers forms the junction primary key in this example. Where needed, those identifiers together with another attribute can form the key.

The important change is the representation of each association as a junction row. Quantity describes that association here, not every occurrence of Product A in every order. Keep both original references visible so the model does not lose one side of the relationship when resolving it.
tb5 A junction table is not simply a copy of one original table. It contains at least both original primary keys as foreign keys, and its key uses their combination or that combination with another attribute. Resolving M:N produces two 1:M relationships.
