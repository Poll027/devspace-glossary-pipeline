Identify a concrete change. The team might agree to clarify instruction examples during refinement and arrange to address that helpful improvement promptly. That is an example action chosen for this situation, not a mandatory retrospective technique.

The useful output is a specific improvement the team intends to address, grounded in its review. A record saying only “communicate better” leaves the change unclear. Keep the discussion connected to what happened and what can improve effectiveness, including whether earlier problems were actually solved. The focus is learning about the team’s way of working, not inventing a new product requirement simply because the team has held a meeting.
tb5 A retrospective is not just a list of complaints or a repeat of the Sprint Review. It examines quality and effectiveness and leads toward concrete improvements. Naming a problem without identifying a helpful change does not complete that purpose.
