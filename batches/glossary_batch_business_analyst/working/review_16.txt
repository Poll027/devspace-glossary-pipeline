tb4 Use an explicit customized workflow. In this example, draft means preparation or review, approved means agreed for delivery, change under review means a proposed revision awaiting a decision, and retired means no longer current. Record R-12’s current status and link it to current work-item status. A proposed revision is not the same as an approved replacement.

Record the affected requirement and change request, review the risks and expected outcomes, and record the approval decision and status. If approved and implemented, document the change and its results. Follow traceability links to identify and update related work. If the requirement is retired, make that transition explicit too rather than leaving an obsolete requirement looking current.

When the requirement lives on a Confluence page, modifying the page creates a new version. Record workflow status in the document, then use More options > Page History to inspect version numbers and compare the revision with the preceding version. Page versions show revisions; the documented workflow status shows whether a revision is draft, agreed, under review, or retired. Keep those two ideas separate.

The BA’s record should let a reader understand the current requirement, the proposed change, the decision, and the resulting work without guessing from whichever paragraph was edited last.
tb5 A newer page version does not by itself mean an approved requirement. Approval, revision review, and retirement are explicit workflow transitions. Likewise, editing requirement text without following its traceability links leaves the related work unaddressed.
