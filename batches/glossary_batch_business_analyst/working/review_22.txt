tb1 Imagine a requirements meeting where one sales representative answers every question before dispatch staff can speak. The facilitator’s task is to support the group’s collaborative process, not to let the loudest contribution become the whole record.
tb2 A workshop facilitator supports the group’s process by planning, guiding, and managing collaborative work.
tb3 Use an agenda whose activities and allotted time support the meeting’s goals. Redirect dominating contributions toward shared goals or invite other participants to balance the discussion. Use silent individual contributions when verbal discussion prevents equitable participation.
tb4 Plan an agenda with activities and allotted time that support the meeting’s goal. For this example, the goal is to clarify delivery-instruction needs, so allocate time for individual input, shared discussion, and consolidating the needs. During discussion, redirect a dominating contribution toward the shared goal or invite others to contribute: “We have the sales perspective; what does dispatch need at that point?” This keeps guidance connected to the work rather than making it a contest between participants.

If verbal discussion still prevents equitable participation, use silent individual contributions. Participants could each write the instruction information they need before the group discusses it. That is an example of the supported technique, not a claim that silent writing is required in every meeting.

The facilitator plans, guides, and manages the process so the group can work collaboratively. The useful result is not merely finishing every agenda item on time: activities and time need to support the goals, and discussion needs room for balanced contributions. Keep the facilitation intervention focused on participation and shared purpose rather than deciding whose requirement is correct on the group’s behalf.
tb5 Facilitation is not the same as speaking most or choosing the group’s solution. Redirecting dominance and inviting input support the collaborative process. Silent contributions are an option when verbal discussion prevents equitable participation, not a compulsory ritual for every workshop.
