tb4 Present the documented goal, scope, requirements, assumptions, and open questions to stakeholders, capturing agreement or requested changes. That addresses validation of the intended business need. Separately verify the written requirement. Check completeness against the documented goals, success metrics, scope, categories, assumptions, and constraints. If the operating conditions are missing, record an open question rather than assuming them.

For ambiguity, ask whether stakeholders interpret “fast” the same way. In this hypothetical case, they clarify the requirement as “During normal dispatch preparation, the current instructions for a selected order are displayed within two seconds.” The condition and threshold are example decisions made by these stakeholders. For testability, identify an executable test: select an order in the stated conditions, measure the time until its current instructions appear, and compare that time with two seconds. The pass/fail result is objective; “it felt fast” is not.

The review therefore has two different outputs: agreement about the intended need, and wording that has been checked for completeness, ambiguity, and testability. Keep unresolved questions visible until the missing information is resolved.
tb5 Approval does not by itself prove that a sentence is unambiguous or testable. Nor does a precise threshold prove the requirement serves the intended business need. Validation and verification address different questions, so one cannot stand in for the other.
