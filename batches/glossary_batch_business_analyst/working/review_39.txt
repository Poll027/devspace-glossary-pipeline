FACT f1 A conceptual data model is a high-level business view of the domain, describing important entity classes, their characteristics and constraints, and their relationships rather than a technical database implementation.
FACT f2 A logical data model is a less abstract, implementation-independent view that details entities and relationships and their attributes, including attribute data types and lengths.
FACT f3 Logical models can specify identifying keys: Oracle Data Modeler allows a primary unique identifier composed of attributes to identify an entity occurrence and foreign unique identifiers that refer to identifiers in another entity.

Classify a high-level business-domain overview as conceptual and a more detailed structure with typed attributes, identifiers, and specified relationships as logical, using its level of detail and implementation independence as evidence.
tb4 Another model details the attributes, including data types and lengths, identifiers, and specified relationships. Classify that as logical when it remains implementation-independent. For example, it might identify CustomerID as an entity identifier and give Name a text type and stated length. In Oracle Data Modeler, a primary unique identifier can be composed of attributes, and foreign unique identifiers refer to identifiers of another entity.

The distinction rests on level of detail, not on whether the model contains business words. Both can describe the same domain. A conceptual overview emphasizes important entity classes, characteristics, constraints, and relationships; a logical model supplies a less abstract structure without being a technical database implementation.
tb5 Logical does not mean a specific implemented database. Both classifications here are distinct from technical implementation. Use the detail and implementation independence as evidence, not the drawing’s title alone.
