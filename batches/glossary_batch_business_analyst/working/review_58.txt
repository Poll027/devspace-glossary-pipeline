FACT f5 Will-not-have items are not needed within the prioritization scope and can be deprioritized or dropped.

tb1 Imagine prioritizing requirements for an initial delivery-instruction release. Define that scope before assigning MoSCoW categories. In this hypothetical scope, seeing current instructions is essential: without it, the solution would not work for its stated purpose, so the group assigns Must have.
tb2 MoSCoW groups requirements as Must have, Should have, Could have, or Will not have for the scope being prioritized.
tb3 Must-have items are essential: without them the solution would not work or the project would be useless. Should-have items are important but the solution can still work without them. Could-have items are desirable but nonessential and have a smaller impact if omitted. Will-not-have items are not needed within the prioritization scope and can be deprioritized or dropped. Define the prioritization scope, discuss each item’s importance, and assign the category the group agrees fits it.
tb4 An amendment indicator is important but the solution can still work without it, so it is a Should have in this example. A convenience color choice is desirable, nonessential, and has a smaller omission impact, so it is a Could have. A vehicle-purchasing feature is not needed within this scope, so it is Will not have. The same labels are not universal classifications of those features; they depend on the agreed scope and importance.

Discuss each item’s importance and assign the category the group agrees fits. Keep the reasoning attached to the category so Must have is not simply a synonym for “a stakeholder wants it strongly.” Should have and Could have both allow a working solution without the item, but the described importance and omission impact differ.

The practical output is scope-specific grouping that distinguishes essential work from important, desirable, or excluded work. Will not have refers to what is not needed within the prioritization scope; it need not be rewritten as a claim that the organization must never consider that item.
tb5 MoSCoW categories are not just four levels of enthusiasm. Must have is essential to a working or useful solution in the stated scope. Will not have is scope-specific, not necessarily a permanent ban. Define the scope and discuss importance before assigning labels.
