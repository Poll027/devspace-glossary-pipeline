tb1 Imagine planned order-service work includes current-instruction access, a customer-search improvement, and broader multi-branch reporting. A roadmap sequences planned work against goals rather than presenting disconnected features.
tb2 A roadmap sequences planned work against goals rather than listing disconnected features.
tb3 Group planned initiatives into a sequence such as Now, Next, and Later, linking the sequence to goals and tracking dependencies. Review the sequence as priorities and information change rather than treating the initial roadmap as fixed.
tb4 For this hypothetical roadmap, place instruction access in Now to support the dispatch-preparation goal, customer search in Next to support faster order finding, and multi-branch reporting in Later to support branch comparison. Track known dependencies as part of the sequence. If later reporting depends on agreed branch information, make that connection visible instead of treating the three labels as independent wish lists.

Now, Next, and Later are an example sequence, not fixed dates promised by this concept. Link each initiative to its goal so a stakeholder can understand why it occupies that part of the roadmap. Then review the sequence as priorities and information change.

If new information changes which work supports the immediate goal, reconsider the initial placement. The useful output is goal-linked planned sequencing with dependencies and revision, not a permanent commitment disguised as a diagram. The example initiatives are invented for the exercise; their particular order is not a universal rule for BA delivery.
tb5 A roadmap is not just a list of features with three headings. The sequence should connect to goals and track dependencies. Also, the initial sequence is not fixed: review it as priorities and information change rather than claiming the first roadmap cannot be revised.
