tb1 Imagine a Tableau source with Sales and Discount. A calculated field derives new data from existing fields without changing the original source. Use Analysis > Create Calculated Field, name the field Discount Ratio, enter the expression, and select OK:
IIF([Sales] !=0, [Discount]/[Sales],0)
tb2 A Tableau calculated field derives new data from existing fields without changing the original source.
tb3 Choose Analysis > Create Calculated Field, name the field, enter an expression, and select OK. The Discount Ratio calculation is IIF([Sales] !=0, [Discount]/[Sales],0): it returns Discount divided by Sales when Sales is nonzero, and zero otherwise. A basic calculation may operate at the row level or at the visualization’s aggregate level, depending on its expression. Numeric calculated fields can be summed automatically when added to a view; change the aggregation if the intended metric requires a different one.
tb4 For hypothetical rows with Sales 100 and Discount 10, and Sales 0 and Discount 0, the calculated values are 0.1 and 0. The expression returns Discount divided by Sales when Sales is nonzero, and zero otherwise. It does not establish that a missing value behaves like zero; this example uses known numeric values only.

A basic calculation can operate at row level or at the visualization’s aggregate level, depending on the expression. Numeric calculated fields can be summed automatically when added to a view, so inspect whether that aggregation fits the intended metric. With rows having ratios 0.1 and 0.2, summing the row ratios gives 0.3; do not label that automatically as the intended business discount metric.

The useful output is a calculated field with an understood expression and a deliberate view aggregation. Creating the field and deciding how to summarize it are separate analytical concerns.
tb5 A calculated field does not modify the original source. Also, a numeric field’s automatic sum is not proof that sum is the appropriate metric. Inspect the expression’s level and choose aggregation for the business question rather than blindly accepting the initial view.
