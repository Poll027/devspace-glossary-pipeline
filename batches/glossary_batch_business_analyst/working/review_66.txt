FACT f1 A user story states a persona’s need and its purpose using “As a [persona], I [want to], [so that].”
FACT f2 Refine oversized backlog items by breaking them into smaller, more precise items with clearer descriptions.
FACT f3 Split larger stories into smaller stories that can be completed within an iteration.
FACT f4 Use INVEST as a quality checklist for each rewritten or split story; if a criterion fails, consider rewording or rewriting the story.
FACT f5 Independent means stories should not overlap in concept and should be schedulable and implementable in any order; check whether a story can be implemented without requiring another story first.
FACT f6 Negotiable means a story captures the essential need rather than a fixed contract of feature details; check that details can be worked out collaboratively with the customer.
FACT f7 Valuable means a story delivers value to the customer; check that each split story describes a customer-valued result rather than only a technical layer.
FACT f8 Estimable means the team can approximate the work well enough to rank and schedule it; check that the team understands the story and its scope well enough to make an estimate.
FACT f9 Small means a story fits within an iteration; check the size of each proposed story and split work that cannot fit.
FACT f10 Testable means the desired result is clear enough to write a test for it; check that the customer can describe how to verify the story, and clarify stories whose result cannot be tested.

tb4 For this hypothetical case, rewrite one story around seeing current instructions and another around finding orders by customer. Each needs a persona’s need and purpose, not merely a technical layer. Check each rewritten story with INVEST. Independent asks whether it can be implemented without requiring the other first and without conceptual overlap. Negotiable asks whether it captures the essential need while details remain open to collaboration. Valuable asks whether it delivers a customer-valued result.

Estimable asks whether the team understands it well enough to approximate the work for ranking and scheduling. Small asks whether it fits within an iteration. Testable asks whether the customer can describe how to verify the result. If the rewritten instruction story still says only “make access better,” clarify it until a test can be described. If it cannot fit, split it further.

The output is not just more story cards. It is a set of clearer, smaller stories checked for all six qualities. The example split is a candidate to assess, not a guarantee of independence or size without the team’s checks.
tb5 Splitting into “database,” “interface,” and “testing” pieces does not by itself satisfy Valuable: each split story should describe a customer-valued result. Nor does using the story sentence format prove INVEST is met. Check each quality and reword or rewrite failures.
