tb4 In Excel for the web, select A1:B3 and use Insert > Charts to choose a suitable type. Give the chart a clear title such as “Order amount by branch” and labels that make the comparison interpretable. The title is an example, not a required naming pattern.

Right-click the chart and choose Select Data. In Data > Setup, assign A2:A3 to Horizontal Axis > Horizontal Field and add B2:B3 under Vertical Axis > Add Field, checking that the category and value fields occupy the intended axes. Inspect the source range and plotted series in the chart task pane, correcting names or ranges as needed. Then use Format to check the chart title, axis titles, and displayed data labels.

The useful output is a chart whose type and field placement answer the business question. Selecting a range and accepting a first appearance is not the same as checking that the chart uses the intended categories and values.
tb5 A chart type should fit the question, not merely look attractive. Bars support category comparisons and lines support trends over time. Also inspect the plotted source and field placement; a clear title cannot fix a chart using the wrong range.
