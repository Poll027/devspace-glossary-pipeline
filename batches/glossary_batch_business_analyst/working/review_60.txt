FACT f1 A weighted decision matrix compares options on criteria whose weights represent importance.
FACT f2 List the criteria and options, assign criterion weights, and score every option against each criterion using a consistent scale.
FACT f3 Multiply each criterion score by its weight and add those products for the option’s total weighted score.
FACT f4 Compare the totals to rank the options; the highest total is typically the best choice under the chosen criteria and weights.
tb1 Imagine comparing two hypothetical instruction-access options on clarity and implementation fit. Assign importance weights 0.6 to clarity and 0.4 to fit. Use a consistent scale where higher scores are better. Option A scores 4 and 2; Option B scores 3 and 4.
tb2 A weighted decision matrix compares options on criteria whose weights represent importance.
tb3 List the criteria and options, assign criterion weights, and score every option against each criterion using a consistent scale. Multiply each criterion score by its weight and add those products for the option’s total weighted score. Compare the totals to rank the options; the highest total is typically the best choice under the chosen criteria and weights.
tb4 Multiply each score by its criterion weight and add the products. A totals 4 × 0.6 + 2 × 0.4 = 3.2. B totals 3 × 0.6 + 4 × 0.4 = 3.4. B ranks higher under these chosen criteria, weights, and scores.

The highest total is typically the best choice under the chosen setup, not a universal fact that B is best in every context. The weights represent importance, and the scores express performance on the consistent scale. Record them so another reader can understand the comparison rather than seeing only the final total.

The practical output is an explicit weighted comparison of options. If a stakeholder challenges the recommendation, return to the criteria, importance weights, and scores. Do not defend 3.4 as if it were independent of those choices. The example weights sum to one; the key calculation is score times weight, summed across criteria.
tb5 Adding unweighted scores is not the weighted-total calculation. Multiply each criterion score by its importance weight first. Also keep the qualification: the highest total is typically best under the chosen criteria and weights, not proof of an objectively best solution in all circumstances.
