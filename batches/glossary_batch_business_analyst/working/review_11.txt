tb1 Suppose the business wants fewer dispatch delays caused by unclear instructions. That broader outcome is a business requirement; it is not yet a detailed description of every action a system must perform.
tb2 Business requirements express the broader business goal or outcome the work should support.
tb3 A requirements document can contain a project summary, goals and success metrics, in-scope and out-of-scope items, requirements, assumptions, and constraints. Organize the elicited requirements in that shared structure and review them with approvers to agree what will be delivered.
tb4 Organize a shared requirements document around the effort. In this hypothetical document, the summary describes the instruction-access problem, the goal is fewer clarification delays, and a success metric is a stated target for dispatch preparation time. The scope includes the North branch and excludes vehicle purchasing. Record the elicited requirements, along with assumptions such as availability of the current instruction record and constraints such as a required approval. These example entries illustrate the shared structure; they do not prescribe the same content for every initiative.

Review the organized requirements with approvers to agree what will be delivered. If an approver questions the branch boundary or success target, capture the issue rather than treating the document’s existence as agreement. The document gathers the business outcome and supporting delivery context in one place so the review has something specific to consider.

A reader should be able to distinguish the reason for the work from the items expected within its scope. For example, “reduce clarification delays” states the outcome, while the requirement entries describe the agreed needs supporting it. Keep that distinction visible instead of replacing the business goal with a long feature list.
tb5 A business requirements document is not made complete by a title or a feature list. The broad outcome and the supporting context need to be clear, and the elicited requirements still need review with approvers. The listed sections are possible contents, not a closed mandatory template.
