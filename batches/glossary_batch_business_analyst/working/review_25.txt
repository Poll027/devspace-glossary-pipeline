tb1 Suppose dispatch work currently begins when an order arrives and ends when preparation is complete. You want a record of that existing behavior, not a cleaner version of what you hope staff will do.
tb2 An as-is process map records the current process rather than a proposed redesign.
tb3 Identify the process’s start and end, collect information about its steps and decisions, and determine their sequence. Represent the observed activities and routing with BPMN tasks, events, gateways, and sequence flows. Review the map with those who perform the process to refine its accuracy.
tb4 Identify the start and end, then collect information about the actual steps and decisions. In this hypothetical process, staff read the order, check instructions, decide whether clarification is needed, request clarification if necessary, and prepare dispatch. Determine the sequence and routing from that information. Represent the activities with BPMN tasks, use appropriate events and gateways, and connect execution order with sequence flows.

If clarification is inconvenient, keep it in the as-is map when it is part of the current process. Removing it because it looks inefficient would change the map from a current-state record to a proposal. Review the map with people who perform the process. They may explain that clarification happens before another step, or that the map omitted an activity. Refine the map to reflect their review.

The practical output is a current-process model with a supported sequence and routing. Accuracy is the immediate aim here. A later improvement discussion can use that baseline, but the as-is map should not silently merge observed behavior with desired changes.
tb5 As-is means current, not ideal. A troublesome step still belongs if it is part of current work. Review with the people performing the process refines accuracy; it is not permission to replace current behavior with a proposed redesign.
