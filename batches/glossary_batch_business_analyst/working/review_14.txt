FACT f1 A use case specifies behavior that provides an observable result of value to an actor or other stakeholder.
FACT f2 An actor represents a role interacting with the subject and may be a person or another system.
FACT f3 Describe a use case’s behavior in natural language with relevant preconditions and postconditions.
FACT f4 Include the basic interaction and relevant variations, exceptional behavior, or error handling.
tb1 Imagine a dispatcher asking an order service for current instructions. In a use case, “dispatcher” is the actor role interacting with the subject; it is not the name of one particular employee. Another system could also act as an actor if it interacts with the subject.
tb2 A use case specifies behavior that provides an observable result of value to an actor or other stakeholder.
tb3 An actor represents a role interacting with the subject and may be a person or another system. Describe a use case’s behavior in natural language with relevant preconditions and postconditions. Include the basic interaction and relevant variations, exceptional behavior, or error handling.
tb4 For this hypothetical use case, state the valued result as obtaining current delivery instructions. Describe the basic interaction in natural language: the dispatcher identifies an order, the service finds its current instructions, and the service presents them. A relevant precondition is that the dispatcher has an order to identify. A postcondition for the successful interaction is that the current instructions have been presented.

Include relevant variations and exceptions. For example, if the identifier is unknown, describe the not-found response. If no instructions are recorded, describe the agreed response for that situation rather than leaving the basic interaction to imply success. The conditions and responses are invented here to show the specification’s structure, not existing tool behavior.

The useful output is an account of behavior that gives an actor or stakeholder an observable result of value, together with the relevant conditions and alternate behavior. A short label such as “view instructions” names an interaction but leaves its behavior unspecified. Conversely, the specification can remain in natural language; this concept does not require programming the interaction.
tb5 An actor is a role, not necessarily a human or a named employee. A use case also needs more than its successful path when variations or error behavior are relevant. Include those behaviors and conditions rather than letting a success-only description stand for every situation.
