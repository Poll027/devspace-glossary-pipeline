FACT f1 Nonfunctional requirements describe how the solution should perform or operate rather than the functions it must provide.
FACT f2 Make the performance expectation measurable by stating a concrete numerical target rather than a vague request for good performance.
tb1 Suppose a requirement says the service must display delivery instructions. That describes a function. A request that the display be “fast” describes a performance expectation, but its wording does not yet supply a measurable target.
tb2 Nonfunctional requirements describe how the solution should perform or operate rather than the functions it must provide.
tb3 Make the performance expectation measurable by stating a concrete numerical target rather than a vague request for good performance.
tb4 For this hypothetical situation, stakeholders specify “Current delivery instructions are displayed within two seconds when a dispatcher selects an existing order during normal dispatch preparation.” The numerical target replaces the vague performance word. The function remains displaying instructions; the performance condition says how the solution should perform when doing so.

Compare the two statements side by side. “Display the instructions” answers what the deliverable must do. “Within two seconds under the stated conditions” answers a question about performance or operation. The two-second threshold is example stakeholder input, not a universal definition of acceptable lookup performance.

When documenting the requirement, preserve the concrete target instead of summarizing it back to “good performance.” The measurable expectation gives the requirements discussion something specific to agree about. In this example, a measured three-second display does not satisfy the stated two-second expectation even if a participant describes it as reasonably quick. That is the practical difference between a numerical target and a favorable adjective.
tb5 Here, nonfunctional distinguishes how the solution performs or operates from the functions it provides. “User-friendly and fast” is not a concrete numerical performance target. Do not confuse a positive description with a measurable expectation.
