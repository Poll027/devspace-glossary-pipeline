tb1 Imagine eliciting requirements for delivery-instruction access. A stakeholder reports that an external address feed may not be ready when needed. Record that known risk together with the blocker or dependency information supplied during elicitation, rather than leaving it outside the requirements discussion.
tb2 Gather information about known risks, blockers, and dependencies while eliciting requirements.
tb3 A risk matrix evaluates a risk using its likelihood and impact. Assess likelihood and impact for each identified risk and use its matrix position to determine a risk level.
tb4 Assess its likelihood and impact. For this hypothetical matrix, the team labels likelihood low, medium, or high and impact low, medium, or high. Suppose the address-feed risk is assessed as medium likelihood and high impact. Place it at that intersection and use the matrix’s agreed risk-level labels to determine its level. The category boundaries are choices for this example; the concept does not supply a universal numerical formula or universal label for that cell.

A second risk might have high likelihood but low impact. Locate it separately rather than assuming frequency and consequence are the same thing. The matrix evaluates both dimensions, so your record should preserve the two assessments as well as the resulting level.

The useful requirements output is a list of identified risks, blockers, and dependencies with reasoned likelihood and impact assessments. It is not a promise that a risk will occur. Nor is merely drawing a grid enough: the assessment and the matrix position must be connected to each identified risk.
tb5 Likelihood is not impact. A frequently occurring inconvenience and a less likely severe disruption can occupy different matrix positions. Do not invent a universal score from category words; use the risk-level interpretation of the matrix being used.
