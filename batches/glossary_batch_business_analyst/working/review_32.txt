The attribute belongs to the thing it describes. A requested delivery date describes an order in this example, while customer name describes a customer.

Read another sentence: “Dispatch staff need the instruction text for an order.” It adds a descriptive need for Order rather than automatically introducing a new entity called InstructionText. The question is which business thing needs describing and which properties are needed for that description.

The output can remain a plain list of entities with attributes. This step does not require keys, relationship symbols, or a database implementation. Those details come later. Focus first on distinguishing a business thing from a descriptive property of that thing, using the narrative as evidence rather than adding every imaginable attribute. The resulting list should answer what the business needs to describe in this example.
tb5 An attribute is not automatically a separate entity because its name appears in a sentence. Ask whether it describes a business thing already identified. Conversely, do not merge unrelated business things into one vague entity just because they appear in the same narrative.
