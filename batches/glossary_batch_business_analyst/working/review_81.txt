tb3 A spreadsheet pivot table can summarize source data with fields placed in Rows, Columns, and Values. Present the answer prominently and include enough supporting detail for the audience to understand it. Select a table or range, insert a PivotTable, place grouping fields in Rows and the metric in Values, and select an aggregation that answers the business question.
tb4 For this hypothetical source, North amounts are 40 and 60 and South amount is 30. Select the source table or range and insert a PivotTable. Place Branch in Rows and Amount in Values, then select the aggregation that answers the question: a total for this request. The resulting totals are North 100 and South 30. Columns can also structure the summary when the business question needs another grouping dimension, but this simple question does not require one.

Present the answer prominently: “North has the larger total in this extract: 100 compared with South’s 30.” Include enough supporting detail for the audience to understand what was summarized and which source the answer refers to. The stated result is for the hypothetical extract, not a claim about all business activity.

The useful output is a targeted answer supported by a relevant summary. Starting with a business question helps you choose grouping and aggregation; opening a spreadsheet does not itself define what the analysis should accomplish.
tb5 A count, average, and total answer different business questions. Do not accept a Values summary without checking the aggregation needed. Also, the report should state the answer clearly rather than make the reader infer it from a PivotTable alone.
