FACT f1 The Definition of Done describes the state of an increment when it meets the required quality measures.
FACT f2 The Scrum Team follows a shared Definition of Done, at least as rigorous as any organizational standard.
FACT f3 An item that does not meet the Definition of Done cannot be released or presented at the Sprint Review.
FACT f4 Check the completed work against the defined quality criteria and identify any unmet criterion before calling it done.
FACT f5 Story acceptance criteria specify completion conditions for that particular story rather than replacing the shared quality standard.
tb1 Imagine a team has a shared Definition of Done for an increment. In this hypothetical standard, required quality measures include passing the agreed checks and completing the agreed review. The exact checks are invented for the exercise; the shared standard must be at least as rigorous as any applicable organizational standard.
tb2 The Definition of Done describes the state of an increment when it meets the required quality measures.
tb3 The Scrum Team follows a shared Definition of Done, at least as rigorous as any organizational standard. An item that does not meet the Definition of Done cannot be released or presented at the Sprint Review. Check the completed work against the defined quality criteria and identify any unmet criterion before calling it done. Story acceptance criteria specify completion conditions for that particular story rather than replacing the shared quality standard.
tb4 Before calling work done, check it against every defined quality criterion and identify unmet criteria. If one required review remains incomplete, the item does not meet the Definition of Done. Under the stated Scrum rule, an item that does not meet it cannot be released or presented at the Sprint Review.

Now compare that with a story’s acceptance condition, such as “Selecting an existing order displays current instructions.” The condition specifies completion for this particular story. Even if the behavior satisfies that condition, it does not replace the shared quality standard. The learner needs to distinguish story-specific behavior from the state required of the increment.

The useful output is a clear check against the shared standard, not a subjective declaration that the work looks finished. Keep unmet criteria visible and do not quietly lower the standard to match what was completed. The BA can help clarify story conditions while the Scrum Team follows the shared Definition of Done.
tb5 Passing a story’s acceptance criteria does not replace the shared Definition of Done. An item with an unmet required quality criterion cannot be labeled done, released, or presented at Sprint Review under the Definition of Done rule. Do not reinterpret that as merely optional reporting guidance.
