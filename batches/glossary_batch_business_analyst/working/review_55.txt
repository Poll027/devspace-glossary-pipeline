tb1 Suppose the current-state assessment records lengthy dispatch preparation and unclear instruction access. Future-state vision states the objectives and situation the business wants to achieve, with a business outcome and measurable success target.
tb2 A desired-state definition states the objectives and situation the business wants to achieve.
tb3 Express the goal using a business outcome and measurable success target. Write the desired objectives alongside the corresponding business areas in the desired-state column.
tb4 For this hypothetical effort, a desired objective could be “Dispatch staff prepare orders with current instructions, with preparation completed within five minutes in the stated operating conditions.” The five-minute target is an example business decision, not a standard for every dispatch operation. It gives the desired state more substance than “make dispatch better.”

Write desired objectives in the desired-state column beside the corresponding business areas. The instruction-access objective belongs alongside the instruction-access baseline; the preparation target belongs alongside the preparation baseline. Keeping that alignment visible lets readers compare like areas rather than comparing a broad aspiration with an unrelated current note.

The useful output is a future situation described in terms of business outcome and success target. It does not need to name a particular tool as the vision. “Purchase software” states an action, while “staff have current instructions and meet the preparation target” describes what the business wants to achieve. Keep the target’s conditions visible so its meaning is not lost in a slogan.
tb5 A future-state vision is not merely a named solution or a favorable adjective. State the desired business outcome with a measurable success target. It is intended future behavior, not evidence that the business has already reached that state.
