FACT f1 Problem framing clarifies the problem rather than choosing a solution.
FACT f2 Write a concise problem statement identifying who is affected, what the problem is, why it matters, and where it occurs.
FACT f3 State what work is included and what is excluded to make the scope boundary explicit.
tb1 Imagine an operations manager saying, “We need a new order system.” That names a possible solution, but it leaves the underlying problem unclear. For this example, suppose dispatch staff at the North branch cannot find the latest delivery instructions, and repeated clarification calls delay dispatch. Those details let you frame a problem without deciding what to buy.
tb2 Problem framing clarifies the problem rather than choosing a solution.
tb3 Write a concise problem statement identifying who is affected, what the problem is, why it matters, and where it occurs. State what work is included and what is excluded to make the scope boundary explicit.
tb4 A bounded statement could read: “North-branch dispatch staff cannot reliably find the latest delivery instructions when preparing an order, causing repeated clarification calls and delayed dispatch.” It identifies who is affected, what is wrong, why it matters, and where it occurs. The sentence does not promise that a new system is the right response.

Now write the boundary beside it: “Included: finding and communicating the latest delivery instructions during North-branch dispatch preparation. Excluded: changing delivery prices, replacing vehicles, or changing other branches' operations.” These are invented boundaries for this exercise, not general exclusions for every BA assignment. If a stakeholder asks to redesign vehicle purchasing, compare that request with the stated included and excluded work. The useful output is a problem statement and an explicit boundary, not a list of features disguised as a problem.
tb5 “Implement an app” is not the same as clarifying the problem. Reword it around the affected people and difficulty before deciding on a solution. A concise problem statement also does not replace the included and excluded work: both need to be visible.
