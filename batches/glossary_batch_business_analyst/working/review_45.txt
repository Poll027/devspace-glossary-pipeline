INDEX 45 sql.where_having_filtering previews []
FACT f1 WHERE selects input rows before grouping and aggregate calculation.
FACT f2 HAVING selects groups after grouping and aggregate calculation.
FACT f3 Use SELECT group_column, SUM(value_column) FROM table_name WHERE row_condition GROUP BY group_column HAVING SUM(value_column) > threshold; to apply both kinds of filtering.
FACT f4 In comparisons, = means equal, <> means not equal, and < or > means less or greater than.
tb1 Imagine Orders has North amounts 40, 60, and -10, and South amount 30. The business question is “Which branches have more than 90 in positive order amounts?” It contains a row-level condition and a group-level condition.
tb2 WHERE selects input rows before grouping and aggregate calculation.
tb3 HAVING selects groups after grouping and aggregate calculation. Use SELECT group_column, SUM(value_column) FROM table_name WHERE row_condition GROUP BY group_column HAVING SUM(value_column) > threshold; to apply both kinds of filtering. In comparisons, = means equal, <> means not equal, and < or > means less or greater than.
tb4 Write:
SELECT Branch, SUM(Amount) FROM Orders WHERE Amount > 0 GROUP BY Branch HAVING SUM(Amount) > 90;

WHERE retains input rows with positive Amount before grouping. North’s -10 row is excluded, leaving 40 and 60; South’s 30 remains. GROUP BY then creates branch groups and SUM gives North 100 and South 30. HAVING retains groups whose total exceeds 90, leaving North with 100.

The order of those roles matters. The positive-amount condition applies to each input row, while the more-than-90 condition applies to the calculated group total. Describing both as simply “filters” hides that distinction.

The comparison operators express the tests: = means equal, <> not equal, and < or > less or greater than. In this example, > 90 excludes a total equal to 90. Keep the query connected to the business question so the learner can tell which condition belongs before grouping and which belongs afterward.
tb5 WHERE does not filter already-calculated groups in this pattern, and HAVING is not a substitute for the positive-row condition. Filtering inputs can change totals; filtering groups decides which calculated totals remain. An amount threshold and a total threshold are different requirements.
