FACT f1 A star schema separates tables describing business entities from tables storing observations or events.
FACT f2 Dimension tables describe entities and their attributes; their key columns identify rows.
FACT f3 Fact tables store observations or events with dimension keys and often numeric measures.
FACT f4 Choose the fact table’s grain so its rows describe a consistent level of detail.
FACT f5 Connect dimension tables to the central fact table through one-to-many relationships using the dimension’s unique key.
tb1 Suppose a BI model needs to report order amounts by customer and branch. A star schema separates the observations or events from tables that describe business entities.
tb2 A star schema separates tables describing business entities from tables storing observations or events.
tb3 Dimension tables describe entities and their attributes; their key columns identify rows. Fact tables store observations or events with dimension keys and often numeric measures. Choose the fact table’s grain so its rows describe a consistent level of detail. Connect dimension tables to the central fact table through one-to-many relationships using the dimension’s unique key.
tb4 For this hypothetical model, choose the fact grain as one row per order. OrderFact stores order observations, dimension keys, and a numeric amount. CustomerDim and BranchDim describe their entities and attributes, with unique key columns identifying their rows. Connect those dimensions to the central fact table using one-to-many relationships through each dimension’s unique key.

The grain decision keeps the fact rows at a consistent level of detail. Do not mix one row per order with one row per branch-month total in that same example fact table and then describe all rows as equivalent observations. The dimension tables provide descriptive information, while the fact table holds the chosen observations or events and often numeric measures.

The useful mental model is a central fact table surrounded by descriptive dimensions. Keys connect an observation to the relevant descriptions. The example uses numeric Amount; fact tables often have numeric measures, but their fields reflect the observations or events being modeled.
tb5 A fact table and a dimension table are not interchangeable because both contain columns. Dimensions describe entities; facts store observations or events at a consistent grain. Mixing detail levels in the example would defeat that grain decision.
