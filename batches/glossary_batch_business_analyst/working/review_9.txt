FACT f1 A focus group is a moderated discussion that gathers participants’ attitudes and opinions.
FACT f2 Prepare a moderator script with specific issues and goals for the information to be gathered.
FACT f3 Keep discussion on topic, invite all participants to contribute, and prevent one participant from dominating.
FACT f4 Record the opinions and disagreements expressed in the discussion without treating those stated preferences as evidence of actual product use.
tb1 Suppose an order-service team wants to hear how dispatch staff feel about a proposed instruction summary. A focus group is a moderated discussion of attitudes and opinions. Prepare a moderator script around specific issues: whether the summary feels clear, which information participants consider important, and where their preferences differ.
tb2 A focus group is a moderated discussion that gathers participants’ attitudes and opinions.
tb3 Prepare a moderator script with specific issues and goals for the information to be gathered. Keep discussion on topic, invite all participants to contribute, and prevent one participant from dominating. Record the opinions and disagreements expressed in the discussion without treating those stated preferences as evidence of actual product use.
tb4 In the hypothetical discussion, one participant says the latest amendment should be most prominent; another prefers the complete instruction history. Record both opinions and the disagreement. Invite quieter participants to contribute and keep the conversation on the script’s issues. If one participant takes over, guide the discussion so their view does not become the only view captured.

The resulting record might say, “Participants disagreed about showing a summary versus a complete history,” with the preferences they expressed. It should not say, “Dispatchers use summaries during actual work,” because that behavior was not established by this discussion. The group’s stated preferences can inform requirements exploration without being relabeled as observation.

For the BA, the practical output is a supported account of attitudes, including disagreement. The moderator’s script keeps the information goal visible, while balanced participation lets more than one perspective be expressed. The invented design choices in this example are prompts for discussion, not a finding that either choice is the correct solution.
tb5 A focus group does not become evidence of actual product use merely because participants describe what they prefer. Keep stated opinions and disagreements as such. Moderating participation also does not require erasing disagreement to make the record look unanimous.
