tb4 In this hypothetical entry, document “An amended instruction requires supervisor approval before dispatch preparation continues.” Record the operating constraint explicitly alongside the required behavior. If the team assumes that a supervisor is available during dispatch hours, capture that assumption too rather than leaving it hidden in the sentence.

The distinction is practical: a requested function might be displaying or amending instructions, while the governing approval or constraint limits how the work may proceed. Make those assumptions, constraints, and approvals visible in the requirement record.

Read the requirement record back to stakeholders with the approval made visible. A description that says only “Users can amend instructions” omits the stated governing condition in this example. The useful output is explicit required approval and operating-constraint information linked to what the deliverable must do. The supervisor rule is invented for the exercise, not a general policy for order services.
tb5 An approval rule should not be left as background prose while the function is documented without it. Capture governing assumptions and constraints explicitly. Do not invent company policies or a complete business-rule classification beyond what the requirements evidence supports.
