FACT f1 FULL OUTER JOIN returns matching combinations and preserves unmatched rows from both tables.
FACT f2 Columns on the missing side of an unmatched row are filled with NULL.

tb1 Imagine comparing two independently supplied extracts, rather than tables with an enforced foreign-key relationship. Orders contains (10, 101) and (11, 999); Customers contains (101, North Stores) and (102, South Stores). You want matching combinations plus unmatched rows from either extract.
tb2 FULL OUTER JOIN returns matching combinations and preserves unmatched rows from both tables.
tb3 Columns on the missing side of an unmatched row are filled with NULL. Write SELECT a.column_name, b.column_name FROM table_a AS a FULL OUTER JOIN table_b AS b ON a.key_column = b.key_column; to preserve unmatched rows on either side.
tb4 Write:
SELECT a.OrderID, b.CustomerID FROM Orders AS a FULL OUTER JOIN Customers AS b ON a.CustomerID = b.CustomerID;

The matching pair produces (10, 101). Order 11 has no customer match, so it produces (11, NULL). Customer 102 has no order match, so it produces (NULL, 102). These are result contents, not a guaranteed sequence.

For each unmatched row, fields on the missing side are filled with NULL. The join therefore preserves unmatched rows from both tables instead of choosing only the left or the right side. The sample’s unmatched identifier is deliberate reporting data; it would not satisfy a matching foreign-key constraint.

The business interpretation is that both extracts remain represented under the chosen condition, including rows needing further investigation. A full outer join does not itself repair unmatched references, infer missing customer details, or declare every preserved row valid. Its supported role here is returning matching combinations and preserving unmatched rows from either side.
tb5 Preserving unmatched rows is not the same as resolving their missing match. Full outer join supplies NULL for fields from the absent side; it does not invent that side’s data. Matching combinations still appear alongside the unmatched rows.
