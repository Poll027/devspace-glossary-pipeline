tb4 Read each letter as a different relationship to this task. Responsible identifies the person or people doing the work. Accountable identifies the one person ultimately answerable for completion. Consulted participants provide input through two-way communication; Informed participants receive updates through one-way communication. The row therefore does not simply mark who attends a meeting.

Build the chart by listing tasks against stakeholders and assigning appropriate R, A, C, or I entries. For a second example task, “Prepare an instruction-access demonstration,” the role assignments might differ. Do not carry the first row’s assignments over merely because the same people are involved. Check that each task has one accountable owner.

In the first row, asking dispatch for input fits C; sending the sponsor a progress update fits I. If two people are marked A for the same task, the row does not meet the one-accountable-owner check. Resolve the ownership assignment rather than leaving two competing A entries. The useful result is task-specific clarity about work, ownership, consultation, and updates, not a general ranking of stakeholder seniority.
tb5 Responsible and Accountable are not interchangeable. Several people can do the work, while the task has one ultimately answerable owner. Consulted also differs from Informed: input requires two-way communication; receiving an update is one-way.
